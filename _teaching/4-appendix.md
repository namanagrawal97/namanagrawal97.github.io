## Sample Assignments

Each essay assignment runs as a cycle: a first submission graded for completion, a structured peer review whose criteria double as the final grading rubric, and a revised submission with a written revision reflection.

**[Sample Assignment 1: "Frankensteininus Wants YOU (to do research)" (PDF)](/files/Sample_Assignment_1_Frankensteininus.pdf)**

Essay 2 of the course. Students act as research assistants to a fictional Roman scientist and write him a report on ancient Egyptian, Greek, and Roman models of where consciousness resides. They map each model's premises to its conclusion, note the social, political, or religious forces shaping that reasoning, and close with a practical recommendation. Includes the student prompt, peer review worksheet, and revision reflection.

**[Sample Assignment 2: "A Letter Across Time" (PDF)](/files/Sample_Assignment_2_Letter_Across_Time.pdf)**

Essay 3 of the course. Students write a letter from one of Galen, Vesalius, or Descartes to another, defending the author's model of the brain, engaging with the recipient's ideas, and revealing a blind spot the author could not have known about. They close with a short reflection in their own voice on how scientific ideas change. Includes the student prompt, peer review worksheet, and revision reflection.
