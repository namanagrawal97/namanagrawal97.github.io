### Selected student comments

> Conferences definitely helped me recognize what was and wasn't working in my writing because the one-on-one created a judgement-free, focused environment... Peer review also heavily helped me because my peers caught many confusing parts in my writing that I would not originally have caught, being the reader of my own writing. I could definitely tell that after making edits based on peer revision, my writing was a lot clearer and my arguments were stronger because they addressed counter-arguments my peers brought up.

> I think that in particular, the emphasis on the graded components of the class being the revised essays was really useful in terms of forcing me to focus on revising the work and making me really think about the way that I go about the revision process.

> In individual conferences, gave actual advice on how to do the writing process over the course of a week that works with how my brain works. Slides were funny. Instructor was relatable. Instructor has good handwriting and drawing skills. Instructor is very attentive and nonjudgmental.

> I thought the Friday activities were actually pretty nice because they made the class feel less awkward over time. I definitely felt more comfortable talking during discussions and peer review once I knew people a little better instead of feeling like I was just talking to strangers.

**Constructive feedback**

> Distributing the time spent on each century (content wise) more evenly or more focused on modern approaches of neurobiology would have been more beneficial to my understanding of the inner working of the brain and different theories. This is because we focused too much on early civilizations approach to the brain.

> Some improvements that could be made would be to make this a larger class. A larger group of students would help with having big group discussions and facilitate my learning a little more with peers.

> Honestly the only thing I didn't like was presenting in class. We only did it once but I didn't feel like it was conducive to learning.
